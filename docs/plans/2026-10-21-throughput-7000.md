# Throughput programme: 7,000 products (35,000 photos, 4 channels, 140,000 images) in about 2 hours

Date 2026-10-21. Plan only: nothing changed, deployed or run; no secrets read. Repo `main` cbaeaca.
Live shape read with `gcloud run services describe`, `tasks queues describe`, `sql instances describe` and
`gcloud alpha quotas info`. Every number is marked (M) measured, (C) from code or describe, or (A) assumption
to be measured in the rehearsal. Sources: `scratchpad/investigations/{concurrency-and-timing,
s3-performance-1000,s3-concurrency-limits,enhancement-cost-delta}.md`, docs/solutions/db-connection-budget.md,
docs/solutions/go-live-retrospective.md, docs/PROGRESS.md rows 46 to 50, docs/SPEC.md (pipeline, locks,
data model), the code named per change below.

## 0. One page for the owner

What the run needs: about 6 photos per second sustained for 100 minutes (1,400 images per minute); today the
stack does about 100 images per minute (M, load c66272e7). Nothing about the product (paths, locks, one batch
per product, the QA judge, the sheet) changes; what changes is how many photos are worked on at once and how
each resource is kept busy.

What changes (code, in parallel worktrees, 5 workstreams, about 3 working days of Opus build time plus 1 day
of review, merge and deploy):
1. Each chunk task works on N photos at once (N = 4) instead of one, and writes its results to the database
   in groups per product instead of per output.
2. Stages get their own queues (cut-out, render, check) so the GPU, the worker CPU and the judge are busy at
   the same time instead of in three waves. Small operator batches keep today's queue, so they are never
   behind a bulk run (this is the fair sharing).
3. The GPU service keeps up to 3 models resident (today one; 71% of GPU time was reloads, M) and the worker
   can use a second GPU service in Mumbai; the upscaler of spec 11 gets its own GPU service at this scale.
4. The judge gets a real retry with backoff and jitter, a global in-flight cap derived from the queue shape,
   and optional provider routing; Vertex AI direct is prepared as an option the owner can switch on.
5. Start, finalize and the sheet are made to work at 7,000 products (today Start would time out at about
   1,400 serial enqueues, and finalize re-checks 140,000 links inside one 900 s task).
6. A throughput line (images per minute, in flight, queue depth) on the batch page and in the load report.

What it costs: per 7,000-product run about USD 40 to 55 billed (GPU 12 to 20, worker 9 to 12, judge 10 to 12,
cross-region transfer 3 to 5, storage and tasks about 1), of which about USD 26 is counted against the daily
cap today (judge 10 plus recorded compute 16, the latter double counted). Always-on delta: Cloud SQL one tier
up, db-f1-micro to db-g1-small, about USD 20 a month (A, verify on the pricing page). Nothing else is always on.

What the owner must approve: (1) the Cloud SQL tier change (restarts ila-pg for a few minutes, shared with
ila-web and ila-worker; needs a maintenance window and an edit to the AGENTS.md cost rule); (2) the L4 quota
request 3 to 12 in asia-southeast1 (Google approval, 1 to 3 business days, may be refused); (3)
DAILY_SPEND_CAP_USD 10 to 60 on the run day (and permanently if 1,000-product batches become normal);
(4) whether to add Vertex AI direct as a judge transport (service account auth, no new secret; enables
`aiplatform.googleapis.com`, role roles/aiplatform.user for catalog-worker@); (5) the two rehearsal windows
(nobody on the site, about 1 h and about 2.5 h) and the 7,000 run window.

Expected time for 7,000 products (typical; low to high in brackets, see section 1):

## 1. Target and capacity model

Target: 7,000 products x 5 photos = 35,000 photos; x 4 channels = 140,000 images. Budget 120 min, of
which 20 min for ramp-up (GPU cold start 30 to 60 s, queue fill) and the tail (last chunks, publish of the
last products, finalize): 100 min of steady state = 350 photos/min = 5.8 photos/s = 1,400 images/min.
Hard rule: the slowest stage must do 5.8 photos/s on its own, and the stages must overlap.

Constants (per photo unless stated): GPU inference warm 0.71 s avg (M, 1,153 calls), 4 to 8 s with a model
reload (M); guard second cut-out on about 15% of photos (A, from B4 "guard-heavy" 8.2 s vs 5.7 s); worker
cut-out slot time 5.7 s today (M) of which about 1.5 s is CPU (decode, resize, PNG, upload) (A); classifier
2.3 s per product (M); render 0.75 s per image at about 1 vCPU (M); judge 1.23 calls per photo, p50 3.2 s,
p95 6.2 s (M); 11% HTTP 429 at 6 concurrent (M); publish 5 s per product (M); finalize tail 1 min at 12
products (M), unknown at 7,000 (A, section 2h).

| Stage | Need at 5.8 photos/s | Today | Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Phase 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPU cut-out | 5.8 x (0.71 x 1.15 guard) = 4.7 L4-s per s; at 70% busy 6.8 L4 warm; 15 to 20 L4 if reloads stay at 71% | 3 L4, reloads: about 1.0 to 1.5 photos/s (M: 63 to 90 photos/min) | 3 L4 warm: 3.5 photos/s | 3 + 3 (Mumbai): 7 photos/s | 12 + 3: 17 photos/s |

Bottleneck order: today (1) the 6 slots from the connection budget, (2) judge 429s, (3) GPU reloads, (4) the
USD 10 cap (a 7,000 run counts about USD 26, so the cap stops the QA after about 2,700 products).
After phase 1: (1) worker CPU (8 vCPU), (2) judge in-flight 24, (3) GPU 3 L4 warm. After phase 2: (1) GPU
6 L4 at 7 photos/s minus ramp, (2) judge 40, (3) DB CPU on the shared core (A). After phase 3: (1) judge
in-flight cap and provider 429 behaviour, (2) finalize and publish tail, (3) worker CPU.
Time model after phase 3: cut-out 35,000 / 17 per s = 34 min, overlapped; render 140,000 x 0.75 / (64 x 0.7)
= 39 min, overlapped; judge 43,000 x 3.6 s / 40 = 65 min (p95 case 43,000 x 4.4 / 40 = 79 min); publish
tail 5 to 8 min after the last check; finalize 5 to 10 min (A). Total about 85 to 100 min typical, 125 min
high. The judge is the stage to probe first (section 5).

## 2. Design changes

### 2a. N photos in flight per task (bounded in-task concurrency)
Files: `apps/worker/src/catalog/chunk.ts` (the `for (const source of sources)` loops of the matte and render
stages become `mapLimit(sources, N, ...)`; the render stage keeps its product-wide `planProduct` step before the
per-photo part), `apps/worker/src/catalog/qa-stage.ts` (`runQaStage` judges N photos of a product at once; the
per-photo `judge(row, photoChecks)` chain stays sequential inside a photo so the second-opinion logic is
unchanged), `apps/worker/src/catalog/deps.ts` (`CatalogDeps.photoConcurrency` from env), `packages/core/src/
catalog/ingest/local-files.ts` (`mapLimit` already exists; move it to `packages/core/src/concurrency.ts` and
re-export). New env `CATALOG_PHOTO_CONCURRENCY` (default 1 = today's behaviour; the run uses 4), set in
`infra/deploy.sh` and in the smoke job env (2) so the smoke exercises the parallel path.
Guarantees: the chunk claim and `underClaim` (SELECT FOR UPDATE of the chunk row) are unchanged, so ownership
is still checked on every write; N photos share the task's one pooled connection (pool 2 per instance,
concurrency 2), so the budget formula is unchanged; locks and `assertWithinProduct` are untouched (matte and
staging writes stay under the batch prefix; publish is not changed). `mattingMs` and `renderMs` become
atomic adds. Memory: N=4 photos at 12 MP decoded RGB (36 MB each) plus 4 renders is under 600 MB; the
worker goes to 4 GiB in phase 2 anyway (A, measure RSS in the rehearsal).
Tests: pipeline.test.ts gains a case with N=3 where one photo fails decode and the other two render and
publish (one failure never blocks the rest); a case that an ownership change mid-flight aborts all N; the
cost row sums match the sequential run. Effort: 1.5 days.

### 2a'. Stage queues, bulk routing and Start at scale
Files: `packages/core/src/tasks.ts` (`queueForRoute(route, env, stage?)`: routes `/tasks/catalog/chunk` with
body.stage to `TASKS_QUEUE_<STAGE>` when set and the batch is bulk; `/tasks/catalog/publish` to the render
queue of bulk batches), `apps/worker/src/catalog/chunk.ts` (the three `deps.enqueue` calls pass the stage and
the bulk flag), `apps/web/lib/catalog/start.ts` and `apps/worker/src/catalog-load.ts` (chunk rows inserted
with one multi-row insert; the 1,400 enqueues move out of the web request: Start enqueues one
`/tasks/catalog/start` task, new route in `apps/worker/src/catalog/start.ts`, which enqueues the chunk tasks
16 at a time and re-enqueues itself with a cursor; today's serial loop is 0.16 s per task (M) = 224 s inside
the 300 s web timeout). Bulk = `counts.products >= BULK_BATCH_PRODUCTS` (env, default 100) or `isTest`.
Queues (created idempotently in deploy.sh like `$QUEUE-ingest`): `catalog` (operator batches, unchanged),
`catalog-bulk-matte`, `catalog-bulk-render`, `catalog-bulk-qa`. Check in deploy.sh: `catalog + ingest +
bulk-matte + bulk-render + bulk-qa <= WORKER_MAX_INSTANCES x WORKER_CONCURRENCY`.
Guarantees: a task is still one chunk at one stage with the same task id scheme (`chunk-<id>-<stage>`), so
dedup, the sweeper's recovery and the Start re-enqueue path work unchanged; the smoke uses one queue
(bulk env unset). Tests: tasks.test.ts routing table; start task cursor and idempotence. Effort: 1 day.

### 2b. GPU: residency, memory, concurrency, instances, the upscaler
Files: `services/matting/app.py` (`SESSIONS` becomes an LRU of `MAX_RESIDENT_SESSIONS` (env, default 3 on
GPU, 1 on CPU); `create_session` passes CUDA provider options `arena_extend_strategy=kSameAsRequested` and
`gpu_mem_limit=<GPU_MEM_LIMIT_BYTES>` (env, default 6 GiB) so one session's arena cannot grow into the
others (the README records exactly that failure: an 822 MB arena allocation failed after 100 inferences);
PNG decode and encode move outside `INFERENCE_LOCK`; `/health` adds `resident: [...]` and a `gpu` block
with used and total VRAM read through `pynvml` or `nvidia-smi`), `services/matting/README.md` (numbers),
`infra/deploy.sh` (`--concurrency=2` on the GPU service so upload, decode and encode overlap with the next
inference; `--max-instances` from `GPU_MAX_INSTANCES`, default 3), `packages/core/src/catalog/matte.ts`
(accept `MATTING_GPU_URLS` as a comma list; one breaker per service; round robin among healthy GPU services
with the same model; an in-process semaphore `MATTE_INFLIGHT_PER_INSTANCE` (default 4); HTTP 429 and 503 from
the GPU service are back-pressure: wait 1 to 3 s with jitter and try the next service, counted as a breaker
failure only after 3 consecutive waits), `apps/worker/src/catalog/deps.ts` (construct from the list).
VRAM (A, to measure with the new /health in the rehearsal; file sizes are known): birefnet-general and
birefnet-hrsod about 0.9 GB fp32 weights each, bria-rmbg about 0.9 GB, birefnet-general-lite about 0.2 GB;
working memory at 1024 x 1024 about 2 to 4 GB per session with the arena capped. Three resident sessions at 6
GiB cap = 18 GiB of the L4's 24 GB (22.5 usable). If the measurement shows more, LRU 2 is the fallback.
Batch inference: rembg's `remove()` is one image per call and the ONNX exports have batch 1 (C); gain on an
L4 at 1024 px would be about 1.3x (A); not in scope, noted as a later option.
Instances: phase 2 uses two GPU services, `catalog-matting-gpu` (asia-southeast1, max 3) and a new
`catalog-matting-gpu-mum` in asia-south1 (same image and flags; the quota `NvidiaL4GpuAllocNoZonalRedundancy
PerProjectRegion` lists asia-south1 as applicable, so a separate default of 3 (A, verify with the deploy; the
GPU block in deploy.sh already tolerates a quota refusal with a WARN)). This also removes the cross-region
transfer for half the photos. Phase 3 raises asia-southeast1 to 12.
Upscaler (spec 11): at this scale a Trendyol or Centrepoint channel adds 1 to 2 upscale calls per photo at
about 1.5 s (A), 15 to 30 GPU hours per run, as much as the cut-out itself. Recommendation: deploy
`/upscale` as its own service `catalog-upscale-gpu` from the same image (env `MATTE_SERVE=upscale|matte|
both`), with its own max-instances and VRAM, so the upscaler never evicts a resident matte session and
the quota request names both (matte 12, upscale 4 to 6 if any triggering channel is in the run; the 7,000
run as specified, 4 P1-style channels, needs none). Tests: Python tests for LRU eviction order, the memory
cap option and the lock scope; matte.test.ts for round robin, per-service breakers and the 429 wait.
Effort: 2 days (1 service, 1 client).

### 2c. Judge: limiter, retries, provider routing, Vertex option, two models
Files: `packages/core/src/llm/openrouter.ts` (retries on 429, 5xx, timeout and network: attempts 1 to 5 with
waits 2, 4, 8, 16 s times a jitter of 0.5 to 1.5, `retry-after` honoured up to 30 s; today one retry at
line 72), `packages/core/src/llm/limiter.ts` (new: a per-process semaphore `LLM_INFLIGHT_PER_INSTANCE` plus
AIMD: on a 429 the instance halves its permits for 60 s, then adds one back per minute), `packages/core/src/
llm/llm.ts` (`callLlm` takes the permit; optional `provider` body field from `OPENROUTER_PROVIDER_ORDER` and
`OPENROUTER_ALLOW_FALLBACKS`, default unset = OpenRouter's own routing), `packages/core/src/llm/vertex.ts`
(new, optional transport behind `LLM_TRANSPORT=openrouter|vertex`: `google-auth-library` ADC token of the
worker service account, `generateContent` on the global endpoint, same request and response mapping into
`ChatCompletionResponse`, cost computed from token counts and a price table in cost.ts since Vertex returns no
`usage.cost`), `infra/deploy.sh` (env), `apps/worker/src/catalog/qa-stage.ts` untouched (the limiter sits
inside `callLlm`; `catalogLlmDeps` still checks the lease and cap before each attempt, so retries never pass
the cap). Global cap = qa dispatches x N photos x per-instance permits is bounded by the queue shape; the
deploy script prints "judge in flight at most X" next to the connection budget.
Provider facts (verify with Context7 before build, per AGENTS.md): OpenRouter has no fixed rate limit on paid
models; the 429s were upstream capacity for the default judge (Google); OpenRouter's `provider.order` and
`allow_fallbacks` let the call fall to a second provider of the same model when one is rate limited, and a
second provider of a Gemini model is still Google capacity. Vertex AI direct: pay as you go under dynamic
shared quota (no fixed RPM, still 429 under contention), list price for the same model (no OpenRouter credit
fee, about 5%), auth by service account (no new secret type), needs `aiplatform.googleapis.com` and
roles/aiplatform.user for catalog-worker@ at project level (infra/*.sh, idempotent). Provisioned Throughput
is a monthly commitment: not for one run. Recommendation: build the limiter and retries now; keep OpenRouter;
prepare the Vertex transport as a switch; probe both in the judge test (section 5) and pick by 429 rate.
Two judge models: the policy already carries `qaModel` and `qaModelSecond` (schema lines 196, 205); spreading
checks over two models means `qaModelSecond` on the primary call too, chosen per photo round robin when the
first model's 429 rate is high. The no-model-name rule holds: model ids live in model_policy, env and logs
only; UI copy stays "the quality check"; `catalog_outputs.qa_model` is stored, never shown. Caution: two
judges with different calibration change hold rates; only the eval set (spec 10) can approve a second
primary model, so this is an option, not part of the run. Tests: openrouter.test.ts backoff schedule, jitter
bounds, retry-after cap, no retry on 4xx other than 429; limiter.test.ts AIMD; vertex.test.ts mapping with a
fake fetch; judge.test.ts unchanged. Effort: 1.5 days, plus 1 day for the Vertex transport.

### 2f. Fair sharing
By class, not by dispatcher: operator batches stay on `catalog` with their own 6 dispatches, which the bulk
queues never use; a bulk batch never enqueues on `catalog`. During the 7,000 run an operator's 10-product
batch gets the same 6 slots it has today (7 min, M) while the GPU, judge and DB are shared: expect about 1.5x
slower (A). The load driver's guard stays (it refuses to start while any batch is mid-pipeline) and gains a
re-check every 5 minutes that logs operator batches created during the run (PROGRESS row 47 asks for it).
A per-batch share inside the bulk queues (two bulk batches at once) is out of scope: the run is one batch.
Files: tasks.ts routing (2a'), catalog-load.ts. Effort: included in 2a'.

## 6. Risks and bounds
| Risk | Bound |
|---|---|
| L4 quota refused or late | Phase 2 still gives 6 L4 (Singapore 3 + Mumbai 3): 2.4 h typical; a 4,000-product batch fits 2 h; the run is split into two batches on two days if the owner needs 2 h strictly |
| Judge provider capacity (429 above 5% at 40 in flight) | AIMD drops the in-flight count instead of failing checks; unavailable checks still hold outputs for attention (today's behaviour), never publish unjudged; options in order: provider order, Vertex direct, two judge models; the probe decides before the run |
| DB saturation (shared core at 25 transactions per second plus publish) | grouped writes; the retrying pool; the rehearsal watches CPU and backends; fallback tier db-custom-1-3840; worst case lower BULK_* dispatches by env and redeploy (no code) |
| Cloud Run cold starts at scale (16 workers, 12 GPUs at once) | GPU instances start in 30 to 60 s (M) and stay up for the run; worker starts are seconds; the queue fill takes the first 2 to 3 minutes and is inside the 20-minute allowance; min-instances stay 0 |
| Cost runaway (a loop of retries, fallback pricing, a stuck chunk) | the daily cap (60) stops the judge; the chunk retry stays at 5 attempts; the breaker on the GPU; the load driver's timeout cancels and cleans up; GPU max-instances is the hard GPU bound (USD 15 per hour at 12) |
| Operator batches during the run | separate `catalog` queue keeps their 6 slots; shared GPU and judge slow them about 1.5x (A); the load driver logs any operator batch created during the run; the owner is told the window |
| Partial failure and resume | unchanged contracts: a chunk that fails 5 times fails its products only; the sweeper requeues expired leases; publish is idempotent; a stopped run can be re-started per product from the attention tab; a crashed finalize re-runs from its own task |
| Oversubscribed GPU (40 calls in flight over 6 L4) | the client's in-flight semaphore and the 429 wait; cut-out slot time grows, nothing fails; the matte breaker opens only on real failures |
| Memory on the worker at N=4 | 4 GiB instances; the rehearsal records RSS; N is an env knob |
| The finalize task at 7,000 products | split into tasks with a 30-minute deadline; the rehearsal at 1,000 measures its scaling before the run |
